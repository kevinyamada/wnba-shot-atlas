    Shot locations

    Just inside the three-point line

    There are far fewer attempts just inside the three point line than just outside it. These long two point shots offer less reward than a nearby three.

    8×higher shot density in the two foot band outside the line

    508 attempts inside versus 4,380 outside, adjusted for area. Game bootstrap interval: 7.27 to 8.83×. This describes shot selection; it does not prove that players stepped backwards.

    Model comparisons

    How well do the models predict makes?

    A Bayesian model shares information between nearby locations. Player effects let shooters differ, while keeping estimates for players with few shots closer to the league distribution.

    The earlier GP fits passed their sampling checks. That tells us the computation behaved well, not that the model must be right. We also need predictions on games the model did not fit.

    The equal weight ensemble has the lowest error in the later regular season sample. The gain is small, and that sample has already been inspected during development. It is a benchmark result, not proof of performance in a new season.

    Spatial validation

    Testing on a different part of the court

    Predicting a new game and predicting in an unseen area are different tasks. For the spatial check, we hold out one of four court regions, then remove training shots close to the held out region. Increasing this buffer makes the test stricter.

    Notes on the methods

    Bayesian models. A prior says which surfaces are plausible before observing shots. The binomial likelihood describes makes out of attempts. Their combination gives a posterior distribution.

    IDW and kriging. IDW averages nearby estimated rates. Kriging uses spatial covariance; universal kriging also includes a distance trend. These are comparison methods here, not full Bayesian shot models.

    SPDE and the mesh. A smooth random field is represented by values on a triangular mesh. Our binomial SPDE fit uses a fast conditional Laplace approximation. Its uncertainty is narrower in scope than the GP's full posterior.

    Ensemble. Average the distance baseline, SPDE, and context boosting predictions. Extra complexity only helps if evaluation supports it.

    Pooled Brier scores across four folds, weighted by shot counts. This diagnostic uses the early season training sample. Settings are fixed before fitting each spatial fold. Games can occur on both sides: it tests location transfer, not future game performance. Buffers are a sensitivity study, not a claim that all dependence ends after four feet.

    Mesh refinement changed some local SPDE predictions. The mesh is an approximation, not a physical boundary on the court. Kriging's transformed rate likelihood is also an approximation. See the methods note for parameter choices and checks.

    What I haven’t modelled yet

    A make probability is conditional on a shot being taken. Expected threat also needs reliable possessions and nonshot actions. The updated source passes the score sequence check, but possessions still need validation. A play by play feed does not record every pass and movement, so ten rows are not necessarily ten basketball actions.

    This is an exploratory WNBA study, not an official league product or a definitive player ranking. The public release was checked against saved game, team, and player region totals. Raw player tracking and defensive pressure are not available.
